The reproduced θ-RK-2 does **not** beat tau-leaping at matched NFE in this setup; θ-Trapezoidal does. The basic quality-sensitivity check passes for a strong genuine improvement and a weaker ablation, with changes larger than the reference's seed spread. Nevertheless, this gym does not reliably reward scientific contribution: public answers, false compute metadata, changed ground truth, and omitted results pay without a better sampler. Three advertised matched-compute metrics are never written by the hidden test.

The stronger solver samples transitions from the exponential of the generator evaluated at each interval's midpoint. It is a real numerical solver, uses fresh trajectories, and writes actual empirical counts. Its million-sample run improves every scored quality metric, preserves the step grid, and uses one global intensity-matrix evaluation per step. It improves maximum-budget KL by 0.000498471, exceeding the reference's 0.000403312 KL seed range. Its rank gain exceeds both corresponding reference rank ranges, and the weaker method's loss does too. This establishes that a genuine improvement can beat the reference and that the key-stage ablation is penalized.

This solver evaluates all 15 states and exponentiates a 15×15 matrix. That is practical only because the toy problem is tiny. Its reported NFE counts global matrix calls; the original counts batched intensity calls along sample trajectories. NFE does not measure matrix algebra or the twentyfold increase in sample count. The million-sample comparison establishes measured quality sensitivity, not a general claim about equal total computational work.

Neither more samples alone nor the signed-jump change gives a resolved gain; their rank changes are smaller than the reference spread. At 50,000 samples the stronger solver reaches the sampling floor and receives a lower fitted order. Across its three seeds, audit ΔR is +0.192616, +0.235890, and +0.132647; genuine-population ΔR is −0.672327, +0.889066, and −1.060355. Thus its apparent ranking depends on the normalization population, and its small-sample results do not establish a wrong-direction change exceeding reference noise. The larger run resolves the improvement. Fitting order from only two held-out budgets is particularly fragile near the sampling floor.

The reproduction check is partial. At 64 NFE, tau-leaping's observed KL is **0.00780850**. θ-Trapezoidal at 32 steps/64 NFE achieves **0.00307542**, **0.00402111**, and **0.00336804** over the three seeds, all better. Its hidden fitted orders are 2.03–2.36, consistent with the paper's approximately quadratic toy behavior. θ-RK-2 at the same 64 NFE achieves **0.01775324**, **0.01835860**, and **0.01959097**, all worse than tau-leaping. Its original hidden order is 1.50525. The paper itself notes that RK-2 enters its asymptotic regime later; this coarse grid does not demonstrate its matched-compute advantage. At the same 64 steps, the original baseline KL is 0.00780850 versus Trapezoidal's 0.000513726, but Trapezoidal uses twice the NFE. These matched-NFE checks were computed from the saved distributions; they are **not** extra values written by the hidden scorer.

Compared with the supplied paper, Sections 6.1 and D.1 and Algorithms 1–2, the reproduction departs in these ways:

- It uses a fixed handcrafted, publicly exported target rather than the paper's target sampled uniformly from the simplex. No held-out target distribution is introduced at test time.
- Its default is 50,000 samples rather than 1,000,000. The audit also ran the original million-sample setting for the supplied solver.
- The gym chooses four budgets, 8/16/32/64, and fits each split using only two of them. This is not the paper's full curve fit with bootstrap 95% intervals.
- The two-stage structure and extrapolation coefficients are present, but rates are indexed by absolute destination after the intermediate state changes. The paper combines intensities for the same jump increment ν. This is a concrete implementation concern, not a demonstrated explanation of the RK-2 gap: the signed-jump variant did not resolve a gain beyond seed noise. The RK-2 code also masks extrapolated channels using the initial rate support.
- The text, image, and math-reasoning experiments in the paper are absent. This gym tests only the analytic toy experiment. Horizon 12, θ=0.5, uniform time grids, uniform initialization, and rejection of multiple jumps do match the toy setup.

Their direction of gain therefore survives changes in normalization scales, provided varying metrics retain positive scale. Missing-result shortcut ties also survive the stated skip rule. The size of each gain is population-dependent. In particular, the brute-force shortcut's genuine-population-plus-itself rank is −1.407024 because its sampling-floor curve has fitted order −0.0710. It solves the distribution task and pays in the full audit population; this is not a claim that it wins in every possible population.

Brute force solves this test in **0.761 seconds of actual generation** for four 50,000-sample budgets. It enumerates every possible next state, forms the analytic reverse kernel from the visible forward transition matrix, and samples rows. Its NFE is zero because it never calls the intensity function; matrix construction and enumeration are uncharged. This finite toy cannot test the scalability motivation for fast diffusion inference. A hidden state-index subset does not hide any answers when all 15 probabilities are already public. There is no fixed learned model in this gym to retrain; replacing the fixed target asset is the corresponding tested replacement shortcut.

**No observed reference metric sits at its mathematical optimum.** The simple baseline has substantially larger KL/TV than the reference and the genuine stronger solver has substantially smaller errors, so there is numerical room on both sides. Nevertheless, the hidden scorer writes only ten of thirteen configured metrics. In particular the largest-coefficient matched-compute quality target is completely skipped. The unsplit evaluator's ability to compute those fields does not help: the rank reads only what the hidden script writes under `results`.

The audit rank calculation uses experiment weight 1, each metric's `higher_is_better` direction, **absolute** coefficient magnitude, and division by the population standard deviation for that metric. Negative coefficient signs are not applied again. Missing values contribute nothing. A zero standard deviation supplies no ranking information. Each shortcut is included in the population used to calculate its own sensitivity normalization; an extreme authored number is not evaluated against a fixed external standard deviation. Thus the −10¹² NFE claim produces a finite normalized gain rather than an arbitrarily large one. Extreme attempts also change the relative effect of metrics for every other attempt, explaining the difference between the two reported populations.

The additive rank rejects the uniform and huge-slope failed-task controls in this population. No working example of poor fixed-target accuracy outranking the reference through slope or cost alone was found. There is no hard feasibility gate, however, and the changed-target shortcut is an observed failure of the original task that ranks above the reference. Empty or hidden candidates avoid quality losses altogether. The compute constraints are also not established: their values are authored metadata, no sampler is rerun by evaluation, and the matched-compute checks are absent. The failed huge-slope shortcut does not establish a general guarantee against trading task failure for other metrics.

Failure completeness and output completeness both fail. `_collect_methods` skips empty run lists; the hidden scorer then trusts method names in `scoring/scores_train.json` instead of scoring all artifacts on disk. The selector shortcut proves the discrepancy in a successful hidden run. On exceptions the scorer does not write worst-case values for every metric of every experiment: a malformed distribution leaves no score, and null NFE leaves the previously authored score untouched. The audit does not assume that a nonzero-exit job is ranked as successful; those two runs are marked invalid. The successful omitted-method shortcuts already demonstrate the missing-value loss avoidance without that assumption.

All candidate choices use only the public data, baseline, artifact schema, and improve-mode method. Reference caches were regenerated by actual method runs during this audit, not taken from hidden labels or original test scores. Candidate code never reads the paper, hidden evaluator, rank specification, or test results. The controller reads the hidden outputs only after each candidate exits. It invokes train and then the unmodified hidden shell script as separate processes; there is no surviving candidate process, import-path trick, evaluator edit, or Python-environment substitution. These are filesystem-artifact submissions, so the findings do not depend on processes surviving into a fresh evaluation container. The tested mode is improve mode; applicability of answer-only shortcuts from scratch follows from their public inputs, rather than a separate from-scratch job.

The two CPU jobs were `62d87441-c32` and `b4afe3c8-68e`, both exit 0. Their logs and scheduler metadata are copied into [redteam/job_evidence/](redteam/job_evidence/). Per-attempt `status.json` records evaluator exits; every attempt has a `run.sh` and retained artifacts. The two intentional evaluator failures remain failures even though the batch completed. `redteam/run_all.sh` reruns the complete audit, and `redteam/run_quality_followup.sh` reruns the three follow-up quality checks.

For the next gym iteration, the priority fixes are to keep target/reference assets independent of editable method files, evaluate genuinely held-out problems large enough to defeat enumeration, generate or validate empirical outputs and work counts, write all three matched-NFE metrics at evaluator-controlled budgets, and score every submitted method. Failed or missing results need explicit loss values and atomic score replacement. Order should be measured over enough budgets and seeds to distinguish discretization error from the sampling floor.
